Made to Fit · look and feel · round 3 · 12 October 2026

Six looks for Made to Fit, same logo

The m cupboard mark and the lowercase "made to fit" wordmark stay exactly as they are. These options only change everything around them: colour, type, corners, buttons, how measurements look and how photos are used.

Each option shows the real home page hero and the cut list screen from inside the app, using today's copy, so you are comparing the feel, not new content.

Option 1Calm, light and Scandinavian

Birch

Daylight white with pale birch surfaces, one muted sage for actions and a honey tag for measurements. Thin lines instead of shadows. It feels like a well-made piece of furniture: quiet, careful and expensive-looking without being cold.

Option 2Bold, flat and confident

Forest and signal

A deep forest green owns big areas of the page: the header, the hero and the build bar. Cards are white, and a signal yellow is kept for the main action and for measurements. Square corners and 2px borders. It feels sure of itself, which is what a nervous first-timer wants to borrow.

Option 3Precise, technical, reassuringly exact

Drafting table

Blueprint blue panels with a faint 10 mm grid, white paper for reading, and dimension lines used as dividers. Sizes are set in a mono face everywhere. It makes the measuring and the drawings the hero, and says "this has been worked out properly".

Option 4Homely, colourful, about the room

Paint chart

A warm grey ground and a set of soft wall colours, one per kind of build: sage for eaves, dusk blue for under the stairs, clay for wardrobes, mustard for shelving. Actions are plain ink black. It feels like choosing paint for a room rather than using a tool.

Soft and friendly

Option 6Monochrome, spacious, material-led

Gallery

Black and white with lots of space, sharp corners and big photographs. The only colour comes from real materials in the photos (birch ply, painted walls) and one bright yellow measure tag. Captions read like museum labels. It lets the finished cupboard be the star.

My pick

Paint chart. It moves furthest from "generic app" while staying gentle for a first-timer, and it gives each kind of build its own colour, which helps people find their way through designs, cut lists and manuals. The logo sits naturally in ink black. Birch is the safer alternative if you want calm and premium over colourful.

Parts can mix: Soft and friendly's bigger tap targets and progress steps would work inside any of the others on phones.
