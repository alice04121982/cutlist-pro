Four names and four marks for a self-build cupboard app

The app turns a photo of an awkward space into a fitted cupboard you can build yourself: exact cuts, a picture manual and an order for a cutting service. The job a carpenter charges £2,000 to £3,000 for, at the price of the board.

Why change "CutList Pro". "Cut list" is the name of a commodity output, and the space is already crowded with it (CutList Optimizer, OpenCutList, CutList Plus). "Pro" tells a homeowner the tool is for tradespeople. The name describes the spreadsheet, not the cupboard.

Design review of the current site

What the brand change should also fix

Reviewed from the live home page at 1280px and 390px. The product design is strong; the identity layer is the weak part.

High

The mark has no meaning

An orange rounded square with a horizontal bar reads as a "minimise" or toggle control, not a brand. It cannot carry an app icon or a favicon on its own.

High

The name undersells the outcome

The hero says "Snap the space. Get the cuts." The product's real promise is the finished cupboard. Every option above names the result, not the spreadsheet.

Medium

Two orange calls to action compete on phones

At 390px "Start a project" in the header and "Start with a photo" in the hero sit one scroll apart and do the same thing. Make the header button an icon or quiet secondary on small screens.

Medium

Pills everywhere flatten the workshop character

Every button and chip is a full pill, which is the generic SaaS default. The site already has the right materials: mono measurements, dimension tags and birch ply tones. Square-cornered controls (4 to 6px) would match the cut-panel marks above.

Low

The desktop hero floats in empty space

At 1280px the hero is vertically centred with about 150px of blank above and below. Top-align it so the "how it works" section starts in the first screen.

Low

Keep the hi-vis orange

It already means "measured" (the dimension tags) and "act" (buttons). All four boards keep it, and add birch ply as the second colour so the brand feels like a workshop, not an app store.

Marks are vector drafts for choosing a direction. Once a name is chosen, the next step is final drawing, an SVG and favicon set, and updating the header in index.html.
